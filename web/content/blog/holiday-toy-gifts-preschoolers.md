By October, the family calendar starts filling up: costumes, a trip to Grandma's, a preschool celebration, and a holiday wish list that seems to grow overnight. A toy that works for more than one occasion can earn its place in that busy season.

**For a preschooler who enjoys making things, an age-appropriate building set can be a thoughtful holiday gift.** Look for pieces the child can connect, room for pretend play, and a setup the family can realistically store and use. The best fit depends on the child—not the size of the box.

This guide focuses on building play for children ages 3–5, with ideas for Halloween, Thanksgiving, Christmas, and the days at home afterward. We're the XRUFY team, so we'll explain where our multidimensional blocks fit while helping you decide whether this type of toy suits your family.

You do not need a new toy for every celebration. Try the seasonal activities below with suitable blocks you already own, or use them to imagine how a gift might be played with after it is unwrapped.

- **Halloween: Saturday, October 31.** Make a friendly creature or a pretend trick-or-treat neighborhood.
- **Thanksgiving: Thursday, November 26.** Set up a supervised building spot for cousins and visiting family.

## What makes a good holiday toy gift for a preschooler?

Start with what your child already likes to do. A child who makes up stories about little people may enjoy figures and small scenes. A child who repeatedly takes things apart may prefer a construction toy with connections they can manage. Someone who loves balancing and knocking down towers may be happiest with loose stacking blocks.

Before adding a building toy to your cart, check these five things:

1. **Age guidance and small parts.** Read the label for the exact set, especially if younger siblings share the play space.
2. **A manageable first build.** Think about whether your child can get started with a few pieces and a little help, rather than needing to finish a complicated model.
3. **Ways to change the idea.** Can a seat turn into a bus stop, or a creature gain a longer tail? Rebuilding gives the toy somewhere to go next.
4. **The family's available space.** Decide where building will happen and where loose pieces will go when dinner is ready.
5. **What is actually included.** Check the selected version, figures, accessories, and instructions. Pictures of possible builds do not always show the contents of every option.

## October: Halloween building activities without the scary stuff

Halloween play can be silly, cozy, or a little mysterious. Let your child choose the mood. You do not need orange pieces, a special holiday kit, or an exact model to follow.

### Make a friendly monster

Start with a small body and invite your child to add something on each side. Perhaps it has one long arm, a tiny tail, or very unusual feet. With interlocking pieces, try changing the direction of a connection to see how the creature changes.

Ask, "What makes your monster laugh?" Then build something it needs: a chair, a home, or a place to meet a friend. The story can keep going even when the building pauses.

### Build a trick-or-treat doorway

Make a simple entrance with room for a character to pass through. Add another doorway nearby to create a little neighborhood. Take turns being the visitor and the person answering the door.

For a younger preschooler, hold the base steady while they add a piece. For a child ready for a challenge, ask whether the doorway can be made wider without getting taller. Keep real candy and food separate from loose toy pieces.

## November: a Thanksgiving activity for the kids' table

A building activity can give children and grown-ups something to do together while the family gathers. Set it up on a separate play surface, away from hot dishes and food preparation. An adult should stay involved; a tray of small parts is not a substitute for supervision.

### Build a seat for every guest

Choose a few toy characters and build a place for them to sit. Ask, "Who else is coming?" Add a seat, widen a bench, or rearrange the space when another pretend guest arrives.

This is an easy way to give different builders different jobs. One child can make the seats, another can arrange the characters, and an adult can help with a tricky connection. There is no need for everyone's contribution to look alike.

### Make something you are thankful for

Invite each person to build something that makes them happy: a pet, a playground, a cozy place to read, or a wonderfully abstract version of Grandma's house. Let the builder explain it before anyone tries to guess.

End by taking a photo of the creations and putting the pieces away together. If toddlers or babies are visiting, choose a different age-appropriate activity for them and keep the small-part building area separate.

## December: choosing Christmas building toys for ages 3–5

If you are shopping for Christmas gifts for a 3-year-old, start with the manufacturer's age label and the child's current interests. Turning 3 does not mean every toy marked 3+ will be equally easy or appealing. A few successful connections with adult help may be a satisfying first session.

For a 4- or 5-year-old who already enjoys construction, look at what the system lets them change. Can they add sideways connections? Make a space for characters? Take a section apart and try a different shape? Those questions are more useful than assuming a higher piece count automatically means a better gift.

For grandparents and other gift-givers, ask the parent two quick questions: "What kinds of toys are they enjoying right now?" and "Are there any small-part or storage concerns at home?" Include a gift receipt when available, and avoid assuming a new system connects with the child's existing blocks.

### Give a play invitation with the present

Tuck a handwritten card beside the box: **"Can you build a cozy place for two friends?"** After the gift is opened, sit down together and start with one small idea.

Other Christmas building prompts include a pretend gift-delivery stop, a winter village doorway, or a seat for a character waiting for visitors. Adapt each idea to the actual pieces in the set. These are open-ended prompts, not included models or promises about what every version can build.

If your family celebrates another December holiday—or simply enjoys winter play—change the story to fit your own traditions. The same little meeting place can welcome any characters your child imagines.

## Keep the play going after the holidays

The stretch between Christmas and New Year's can be a good time to revisit a gift without the excitement of unwrapping everything at once. Put out a manageable selection of pieces and try one invitation:

- **Build it again, but different.** Give yesterday's creature a new place to live.
- **Make room for one more.** Add a guest to a tiny scene and change the build to accommodate them.
- **Tell the next chapter.** Ask where the characters are going, then build something they need for the trip.

Save a photo before taking a favorite model apart. A photo can make it easier to return to an idea while freeing the pieces for something new. Put loose pieces in a suitable container and store them out of younger children's reach.

## Holiday building-toy questions parents ask

### Are building blocks a good Christmas gift for a 3-year-old?

They can be, if the set is labeled for the child's age and matches their interests and abilities. Check the small-parts warning, consider younger siblings, and plan to help with the first connections. XRUFY is for ages 3 and up, not children under 3.

### Do I need a different building set for each holiday?

No. A general-purpose set can support a Halloween creature, a Thanksgiving gathering, and a winter scene. Changing the story often gives familiar pieces a new purpose.

### Can building toys work for a mixed-age family gathering?

Older children and adults can build alongside preschoolers, but a small-parts set should not be shared with children under 3. Keep the activity supervised and provide younger children with toys appropriate for their age.

### Should I buy a holiday-themed kit or an open-ended set?

A themed kit may suit a child who loves a specific model or following directions. An open-ended set may suit someone who wants to invent and rebuild. Choose based on how the child likes to play, and check what the selected box actually contains.

### Will XRUFY blocks connect with other brands?

Do not assume compatibility between different building systems. Check the product information before combining pieces, and never force a connection that does not fit.

A thoughtful holiday gift starts with knowing the child. Give them something they want to explore, a little help getting started, and room to turn the first idea into their own.
